How to track your job applications

Updated 4 October 2026

Past a dozen applications, memory stops working. Roles blur together, a recruiter calls about a job you can't place, and a closing date slips by. A simple record of every application fixes most of that. Here's what to keep, and a free template to start with.

What to record for every application

  • Job title and company. Use the title exactly as advertised; it's what the recruiter will say on the phone.
  • The posting itself. Save the link and a copy of the description. Ads are often taken down once they close, just when you need them before an interview.
  • Where you found it, so you know which sites are worth your time.
  • Closing date, and the date you applied.
  • Salary as advertised, including whether it's per year, day or hour.
  • Location and workplace: on-site, hybrid or remote.
  • Stage: where the application is now (see below).
  • Contacts: the recruiter or hiring manager, and how to reach them.
  • Next step and its date: a follow-up, an interview, a task to submit.
  • Notes: anything you'll want to remember, like questions they asked.

Stages that work

Keep the stages few, so moving an application takes a second:

  1. Saved: found, not applied yet.
  2. Applied
  3. Screening: a recruiter or phone screen.
  4. Interviewing
  5. Offer

Then two ways out: Rejected and Withdrawn. Keep closed applications rather than deleting them; they show you which kinds of roles move forward.

When to follow up

There's no fixed rule, but a common habit is to wait about a week after the closing date (or after applying, if there isn't one), then send one short, polite email asking about timing. Write the follow-up date down when you apply, so you don't have to remember it.
